Standing before you is one of Corralejo's most meaningful sculptures, dedicated to the people whose lives have always been connected to the Atlantic Ocean.
Created by Canarian sculptor Francisco Curbelo Martín and inaugurated in 2002 during the celebrations of Nuestra Señora del Carmen, the patron saint of fishermen, this bronze monument is a tribute not only to fishermen, but also to the women, children and families who stood behind them.
Take a closer look.
Three figures—a fisherman, a woman and a child—embrace each other around a fishing net. At their feet sits a basket full of fish, symbolising the sea's generosity but also the hard work required to earn a living from it.
The plaque beneath the sculpture reads:
"The municipality of La Oliva pays tribute to the men and women of the sea and to their families, who in difficult times contributed significantly to the development of Corralejo."
Just a few metres away you'll notice another sculpture titled "Mirando al Mar" or "Looking Towards the Sea." It depicts a woman gazing towards the horizon while shielding her eyes from the sun. Together, these two sculptures tell the complete story of a fishing village—not only those who sailed into the Atlantic, but also those who waited patiently on shore for their safe return.
Before tourism transformed Corralejo into the lively destination you see today, fishing was the village's lifeblood. Families depended entirely on the sea, and every departure carried both hope and uncertainty.
Today, ferries, cafés and visitors fill the harbour, yet these sculptures quietly preserve the memory of the generations whose resilience built the Corralejo we enjoy today.
Take a moment to stand beside the monument and look out towards the ocean. Imagine wooden fishing boats leaving before dawn while families watched from the shore, hoping to see them safely return with the day's catch.
Sometimes, the most beautiful monuments are not those celebrating victories, but those honouring everyday courage.
