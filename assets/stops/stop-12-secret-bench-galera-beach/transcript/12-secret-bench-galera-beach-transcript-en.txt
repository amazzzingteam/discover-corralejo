Welcome to one of Corralejo's most photographed viewpoints.

Today, this simple white platform with its bright blue bench is a favourite place to stop, admire the Atlantic Ocean and take in the views towards Lobos Island and Lanzarote. Thousands of visitors walk past every year, yet few realise they are standing on a piece of wartime history.
The structure beneath this viewpoint was originally built as part of Fuerteventura's coastal defence system during the Second World War under General Franco's regime. Although Spain remained officially neutral, a network of bunkers and defensive positions was constructed around the island in case the Canary Islands became involved in the conflict.

Fortunately, these fortifications were never used in battle and were gradually abandoned after the war.
According to local residents and the owners of the nearby Galera Beach complex, this particular bunker later became an abandoned hiding place where local youngsters would gather and occasionally carry out activities that neighbours wanted to discourage. Rather than leaving it neglected, the owners wanted to transform it into something positive.

The local story says they spent a long time trying to obtain permission to improve the area. With little progress, they eventually decided to clean and cover the old bunker, creating this viewing platform and installing the now-famous blue bench.

Whether by design or simply through the affection people quickly developed for it, the viewpoint soon became one of the most photographed places in Corralejo. Today, it is a favourite stop for sunset photos, engagement pictures, family memories and visitors enjoying one of the island's most peaceful sea views.

It is a wonderful example of how a place once associated with war and neglect has been given a completely new purpose.

Before continuing your walk, take a moment to sit on the bench, enjoy the Atlantic breeze and imagine how this same location has witnessed both the uncertainties of the 1940s and the relaxed holiday atmosphere of Corralejo today.
