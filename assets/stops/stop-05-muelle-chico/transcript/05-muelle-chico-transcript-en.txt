Walk slowly toward the pier and stop for a moment. Look at the water beneath you. If the sea is calm, you may even catch a glimpse of the volcanic seabed through the turquoise shades surrounding the quay.

Now lift your gaze toward the horizon. Before you stretches the Atlantic Ocean, the same sea that has shaped the rhythm of life in Corralejo for centuries. This small pier may seem simple, but it was once one of the most important places in the town.

Imagine the fishing boats arriving at dawn, loaded with the day's catch. Imagine the fishermen repairing their nets and the families gathered here, waiting for their loved ones to return. For many residents, this was the place where journeys began and ended.

Listen to the sound of the waves gently brushing against the rocks, the soft creaking of the moored boats, and the calls of the seagulls circling above the harbor. These are sounds that tell a story of the sea, hard work, and tradition.

As you observe this small landing place, you are looking at one of the last surviving traces of the original Corralejo: a simple place, yet one filled with memory and identity.
