Welcome to one of Corralejo's quietest and most surprising green spaces.

At first glance, this may look like a simple local park, but it was actually designed with a much bigger purpose: to become a place where nature, education and recreation come together while celebrating the unique landscape of Fuerteventura.

Opened in 2011, the Botanical Park covers more than 28,000 square metres and was created by the Municipality of La Oliva as part of a major regeneration project. Rather than building a traditional ornamental garden, the idea was to create a space that helps both residents and visitors better understand the island's natural environment. 

As you walk through the park, you'll notice that many of the plants are species that have adapted to survive in Fuerteventura's dry, windy climate. Water is one of the island's most precious resources, so the vegetation here demonstrates how nature has evolved to thrive with very little rainfall.

The park is also known locally as La Tunera, a name inspired by the prickly pear cactus, a plant that has become deeply connected with the island's landscape and history.

One of the park's most distinctive features is the large artificial hill rising in the centre. Follow the gently winding path to the top and you'll be rewarded with one of the best panoramic viewpoints in this part of Corralejo. The spiral design was inspired by the geometric ideas of Spanish abstract artist Pablo Palazuelo, turning the walk itself into part of the experience. 

From the summit, you can enjoy views stretching across Corralejo, the volcanic landscape, the famous sand dunes, Lobos Island and, on clear days, even the neighbouring island of Lanzarote.

What many visitors don't realise is that this park was also intended to become an outdoor classroom. Information panels, themed planting areas and open spaces were planned to help people discover the geology, flora and ecosystems that make northern Fuerteventura so unique. 

Like many public spaces, the park has experienced periods of neglect and later restoration. Community projects and environmental initiatives have helped reintroduce native plants and improve the park over the years, reflecting the island's ongoing commitment to preserving its natural heritage.

Take a moment to slow down here.

Listen to the birds, feel the Atlantic breeze and notice how the colours of the volcanic earth, green vegetation and blue sky come together. It's a reminder that Fuerteventura's beauty isn't only found on its beaches. Sometimes it's hidden in quiet places like this, waiting to be discovered.

Did you know?

The Botanical Park covers approximately 28,275 square metres, making it one of the largest public green spaces in Corralejo. 

Its central hill is around 10 metres high and was designed as both a viewpoint and an artistic landscape feature. 

The spiral pathway was inspired by the geometric work of Spanish artist Pablo Palazuelo. 

The nickname "La Tunera" comes from the prickly pear cactus, a plant that has long been associated with Fuerteventura's rural landscape.

Walk to the top of the hill before continuing your tour. From there, you'll see Corralejo from a different perspective—a landscape where volcanoes, dunes, ocean and town all come together, telling the story of one remarkable island.
