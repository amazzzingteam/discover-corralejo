Now stop for a moment along the pier and look ahead of you. Do you see that volcanic island rising from the ocean? It is Lobos Island, a constant presence for those who live here. Between you and the island stretches a narrow channel of sea, crossed every day by ferries, fishermen, and tourist boats. Now lower your gaze toward the harbour. The boats gently rocking on the water tell a story far older than tourism. For centuries, this was the beating heart of Corralejo. 

Every morning, fishermen set sail from here before dawn and returned with the catch that supported local families. Listen to the sounds around you: the calls of the seagulls, the creaking of ropes brushing against the masts, the engine of a ferry preparing to depart.

These are the same sounds that have accompanied life in the harbour for
generations. As you walk along the waterfront, you will notice how past and present coexist perfectly. On one side, fishermen prepare their nets; on the other, visitors board boats for new adventures toward Lobos or Lanzarote. It is precisely this meeting of tradition and discovery that makes the Port of Corralejo one of the most authentic places in the town.
